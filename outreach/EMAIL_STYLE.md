# Outreach Email Style

Cold emails to restaurant groups are written **by Joseph, as a person**, not as a company announcement. They should read like a short note from someone who actually looked at that business.

**This is a guide to take inspiration from, not a template to fill in.** Every email should be written fresh for the group it's going to, in Joseph's voice, built around what we know about *that* brand. Two emails to two groups should not share sentences. The samples at the bottom show the feel; never copy them.

## What every email needs
1. **Who Joseph is**, in his own words, in the first line or two. Plain, no title-stacking.
2. **Why this group.** Something true and specific about them: how many locations, the kind of food they run, a distributor they use, a recent opening, the city. Pull it from what we know about the brand and make it the reason for the email. If we don't know anything specific, say less rather than invent.
3. **The stat, kept simple.** Typical industry leakage is about 1.8% of invoice totals. Pair it with one plain dollar (or pound) figure so it lands:
   - "Typical leakage is around 1.8%. On $90,000 a week in purchasing, that's roughly $1,600 a week."
   - If we know their size, scale it to them and call it a ballpark ("for 14 locations that's probably somewhere around $X a week, just a rough guess"). Otherwise use a round example group and say it's an example. Don't present a figure as theirs unless it's labelled as an estimate.
   - One sentence or two, no more. No tables, no stacked numbers.
4. **One ask:** the free audit of the last 90 days of invoices from one distributor, with the amounts found. No pricing, fees, contingency %, or SaaS tiers in the first email.

## Voice
- Write like a person who runs a small company, to another person who runs a restaurant group. Warm, direct, a bit understated.
- Vary the opening, structure and length from email to email. Don't use the same greeting, same sentence order, or same sign-off every time.
- ~90-130 words. Short paragraphs. Readable in 20 seconds.
- Subject lines are plain and specific to them, the way a colleague would write one. No emoji, no caps, no "opportunity".
- Speak in terms the brand cares about: the food they buy, the items they run, their distributor by name. Chicken, produce, fish, drop fees, substitutions. Not "solutions" or "platform".
- Don't pretend we've seen their invoices. We haven't.

## Avoid
"I hope this email finds you well", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "AI-powered", "streamline", "leverage", "unlock", "supercharge", "exclusive offer", "limited time", "act now", "synergy", "circle back", "touch base", exclamation marks, ROI multiples, "you'll save X", fake urgency.

## US vs UK
Pick from the prospect.

| | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | $ | £ |
| Spelling | organization, color | organisation, colour |
| Distributors (only name the one they use) | Sysco, US Foods, PFG, GFS, Cheney Brothers | Brakes, Bidfood, Reynolds, Direct Seafoods, Booker |
| Wording | credit memo, locations | credit note, sites |

If the distributor isn't known, say "your main distributor" instead of guessing.

## Follow-up
One only, around five days later, short, in the same voice. Re-state the offer plainly and make it easy to say no. Never "just following up".

## Samples (for feel only. Do not reuse the wording.)

**Sample A, US, group with a known distributor**
> Subject: your Sysco invoices at the Smokehouse
>
> Hi Dana, I'm Joseph. I run a small company called Tenbreads, and I've been going through how multi-unit groups like yours get billed by their distributors. Running 14 locations on one Sysco account caught my eye.
>
> Typical industry leakage is around 1.8%, which on $90,000 a week in purchasing works out to roughly $1,600 a week. It's usually small stuff: a chicken price creeping up a few cents a pound, a substitution billed at spot price, a drop fee that shouldn't be there.
>
> I'd happily go through the last 90 days of your Sysco invoices for free and tell you what I find. Worth a look?
>
> Joseph

**Sample B, UK, less is known about the group**
> Subject: a free look at your supplier invoices
>
> Hello Tom, Joseph here. I check restaurant groups' supplier invoices against their agreed prices, and I'm writing because your group seems to be the right size for it to be worthwhile.
>
> The industry average for overcharges is about 1.8%. For a group buying £60,000 a week, that's around £1,000 a week that mostly goes unnoticed because nobody has time to check every line.
>
> If you'd send me 90 days of invoices from one supplier, I'll go through them for nothing and show you exactly what I find. Interested?
>
> Joseph
