A $45 drop fee on a contract that says "all-in delivered pricing."

You signed the price master. Delivered pricing was the point. Then a fuel charge, a drop fee or a split-case adder shows up as its own line on the invoice, and it gets paid because it is small.

Illustrative math: 12 locations, 2 deliveries a week each, $45 per drop. That is 24 drops, $1,080 a week, about $56,000 a year. (Illustrative numbers, not a client result.)

The insight for ops: the contract decides which adders are allowed. The invoice shows which adders were billed. Nobody compares the two on every invoice, so the difference is never questioned.

A simple check: list the adders your contract allows (fuel, pallet, split-case, drop) and the amounts. Then flag any invoice line that is not on that list, or that is higher than it.
