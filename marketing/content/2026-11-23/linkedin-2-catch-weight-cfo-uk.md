# LinkedIn post 2 - Catch-weight discrepancies (persona: CFO / VP Finance, UK, £)
Status: draft. Owner publishes. Not posted.

A 10 kg box of fish, contracted at £9.20/kg, billed as 10.8 kg. That is £7.36 over on one box.

The extra 0.8 kg is packaging and ice. The invoice used the gross weight of the container instead of the net weight delivered. The line looks normal, so it is paid.

Illustrative math: 12 sites, 2 boxes a week each, £7.36 over per box. That is about £177 a week, or about £9,200 a year, on one product line. (Illustrative numbers, not a client result.)

The insight for finance: meat, fish and cheese are priced by weight, so the weight on the invoice is part of the price. A small weight error is a price error, and it repeats on every delivery.

The check is short: compare the invoiced weight to the net weight on the delivery note, line by line, for every weighed product.
