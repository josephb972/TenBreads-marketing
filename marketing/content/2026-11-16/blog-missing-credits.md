# The credit note that never arrived: how promised credits slip through AP

Status: draft. Owner publishes. Not posted.
Pillar: Missing promised credits. Persona: AP / Finance Manager (UK, £).
Suggested meta description: Why credits promised at delivery often never reach the supplier statement, and a simple weekly routine AP teams can use to make sure every one is applied.

A case of salmon arrives damaged. The driver takes it back, writes "credit 1 x salmon £62.00" on the delivery note, and the site manager signs. Everyone agrees the money is owed.

Then the delivery note goes in a tray in the back office. The invoice arrives in your inbox at full value. You pay it. A month later the supplier statement lands, and the £62 credit is not on it.

Nobody did anything wrong on purpose. The credit simply never became a record anyone could check.

## Where promised credits go missing

Credits are agreed in several places, and most of them are outside AP's view.

**On the dock.** Short deliveries, damaged cases, wrong items, temperature rejections. The driver notes a credit by hand on a delivery note that is often signed in a hurry, sometimes in grease, and filed at the site.

**On the phone.** A site calls the supplier's customer service line about a missing item. Someone says, "We'll credit that." There is no paperwork at all.

**With the rep.** An account manager agrees to put a price error right "on the next statement". The promise lives in an email thread or a conversation.

In each case, the credit relies on someone at the supplier raising it in their system. If that step does not happen, the original invoice stands and nothing flags the gap.

## Why AP does not catch it

Your team works from invoices and statements. A credit that was promised but never raised does not appear on either. There is no line to query, because the line never existed.

Volume makes this worse. A multi-site group can process well over a hundred supplier invoices a week. Matching each invoice to a delivery note, then reading every handwritten note for credit promises, is not realistic for a small team on a deadline.

So the invoices get paid, the statement gets reconciled against itself, and the missing credit stays missing.

## An illustrative example

These numbers are illustrative, not from any client.

A 12-site group receives one promised credit per site every two weeks, averaging £55. That is 312 credits a year, worth about £17,000.

If one in three never reaches the statement, the group has overpaid about £5,700 a year. If half are missed, it is about £8,600. None of it shows up as an error, because nothing on paper looks wrong.

That is one supplier. Groups with separate accounts for fresh produce, fish and dry goods have the same gap on each one.

## A weekly routine that closes the gap

The fix is not complicated. It is a habit of turning every promise into a record, then matching records to statements.

1. **Make a credit log.** One shared sheet with: site, supplier, date, invoice or delivery note number, item, quantity, amount, reason, and who agreed it. Status starts as "promised".
2. **Capture credits at the source.** Ask each site to photograph any delivery note with a credit, short or rejection on it, and add a row the same day. Phone and email promises get a row too.
3. **Request it in writing.** For every row, send the supplier's credit or claims desk a short email with the invoice number, the item and the amount. Mark the row "requested".
4. **Match against the statement.** When the monthly statement arrives, tick off each credit that appears and mark it "applied". Anything older than one statement cycle gets chased.
5. **Hold, do not just chase.** If your terms allow it, short-pay the next invoice by the value of a credit that is overdue, with a reference to your original request. Check your supply agreement first.
6. **Review the log monthly.** Look for patterns: one site, one driver, one supplier depot. Repeated misses are worth raising with the account manager.

Most of this is admin, not judgement. The value comes from doing it every week, for every credit, without exception.

## What a good credit request includes

A request that is hard to ignore has:

- Your account number and site name
- The invoice number and date
- The delivery note reference, with a photo attached if there was a handwritten note
- The item code, quantity and amount
- The reason (damaged, short, rejected, price error)
- A clear ask: "Please issue a credit note for £62.00 against invoice 104582."

Keep the original email. If the credit has not appeared by the next statement, reply on the same thread so the history is in one place.

## When the log gets too long

For a few sites, a spreadsheet works. As the group grows, the log gets harder to keep current, and the step that slips first is usually the statement match.
