# LinkedIn post 1 - Unauthorized SKU substitution (persona: Ops Director / Head of Supply Chain, UK, £)
Status: draft. Owner publishes. Not posted.

You agreed £24.50 a case for the 2.5kg chips. A substitute arrived and was billed at £31.20. That is £6.70 a case, on a line nobody flagged.

You negotiated the nominated supply agreement so prices would hold. Substitutions are the quiet way round it. The usual item is short, the driver offers an alternative, the site signs so service is not affected. The swap is approved on the dock. The price never is.

Illustrative math: 40 substituted cases a week across 12 sites at £6.70 over is £268 a week, or about £13,900 a year. (Illustrative numbers, not a client result.)

The insight: substitution is a pricing problem, not a supply problem. If your agreement says a substitute is charged at the agreed price of the item it replaced, the check is mechanical. Compare every substituted line to the original item's agreed rate. If it does not say that, it is worth adding at the next renewal.
