# LinkedIn post 1 - Unauthorized SKU substitution (persona: Executive Chef / F&B Director)
Status: draft. Owner publishes. Not posted.

Contracted case: $32.50. The case that landed on your dock: $41.80. Same line on the invoice, $9.30 a case higher.

The driver said the usual item was out of stock and offered an alternative. You signed because the kitchen needed product for dinner service. Reasonable call on the dock. The billed price is the part nobody showed you.

Illustrative math: 60 substituted cases a week across a 12-unit group at $9.30 over is $558 a week, or about $29,000 a year. (Illustrative numbers, not a client result.)

Most price-protection clauses say a substitute is billed at the contracted price of the item it replaced. Whether that happens depends on someone comparing the invoice line to the contract. Chefs approve the swap. AP never sees that it was a swap.

Two habits help: note every dock substitution on the delivery receipt, and check the billed price on those lines against the contract before the invoice is paid.
